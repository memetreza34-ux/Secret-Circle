Use case: stylized-concept.
Create ONE cohesive CHARACTER-AND-MATERIAL FOUNDATION SHEET for Secret Circle, an original party-game app. Landscape 3:2.
The attached image of the green chameleon is the SOLE exact ART-STYLE reference. It is not an edit target. Study its precise sculpted head forms, very expressive oversized almond eyes, solid brows, broad curved shapes, plush matte surfaces, restrained tactile detail and clean colorful 3D-cartoon rendering. All subjects on this single sheet must look designed and rendered by the SAME character artist in the SAME production, under the SAME neutral light. This is a casting/model-development sheet, NOT three game illustrations or a collage of different styles.
LAYOUT: one continuous warm-neutral gray studio background and one shared floor, no panel borders, no writing. Two distinct original role-characters stand next to each other, shown from head to mid-thigh at matching scale; small still-life props beside them establish material language.
LEFT: the reference's green chameleon, recognizably the same character design, curled reptilian tail, short purple jacket over dark shirt, reptilian paws, one brow raised and a knowing tight half-smile. Preserve the chameleon skull and muzzle and the characteristic broad surface markings. One iris and one pupil per eye, a consistent deliberate sideways gaze. A blank ivory card is held in one paw.
RIGHT: a stylized ADULT female detective with a memorable sturdy triangular silhouette, medium brown skin, short swept dark hair as a few sculpted masses, a broad blunt cartoon nose, strong expressive sculpted eyebrows, visibly adult jaw and cheek shapes, slightly asymmetrical skeptical closed-mouth expression. Her eyes use the SAME eye-construction language and stylization level as the chameleon's: large clear white shapes, single dark iris/pupil per eye, sculpted lids, shared restrained highlights. She is an animated cartoon person, NOT a pretty realistic human, fashion doll, baby or soft infant. A simple slate-teal trenchcoat with a broad collar, a burnt-orange scarf and plain round buttons communicate detective; no badges, brands, weapons or jewellery. One hand on hip, the other considering a blank card, skeptical glance toward the chameleon. Coherent stylized human hands with five fingers each.
PROPS, on the shared floor or a plain low plinth: a chunky cream blank playing card, a small rounded wooden hourglass with plum frame and amber sand, and a simple folded purple cloth swatch. Props have NO faces or anthropomorphic limbs. The wood, paper, cloth and glass have the same broad modeled forms, soft edge transitions, simplified surface detail and matte 3D-cartoon material treatment as the characters. No food.
Use one soft neutral key light and gentle fill for every subject, equivalent shadow softness, same perspective and shadow treatment, comparable edge sharpness and detail density. Strong shapes and character acting carry identity, not hyperreal textures.
Do NOT make the human more realistic or cinematic than the animal. Do NOT make the props photorealistic product renders. Avoid pores, skin microdetail, textile fuzz, glossy plastic toys, cinematic depth of field, bokeh, dust, fog, magic glow, double pupils, mismatched gaze, extra fingers or limbs. No words, numbers, logos, watermarks, phones, screens, UI, real people, existing franchise designs, alcohol, drinks, violence or weapons.
The result should let an art director judge ONE reusable cartoon world for humans, animals and inanimate objects.
